# Gap — Hidden Gap Detector

## Goal

Expose missing foundations behind claimed competence without relying on trivia.

Prepare five dimensions internally:

1. causal mental model,
2. boundary or exception,
3. diagnosis from incomplete evidence,
4. transfer to a changed context,
5. tradeoff under constraints.

Ask one main question at a time using stable `Q1` through `Q5`. Do not display a five-row matrix at the start.

After each answer use:

```text
판정: 맞음 / 일부 맞음 / 핵심 오류
확인된 부분: ...
드러난 기초: ...
다음 Qn: ...
답변 틀(선택): ...
```

A repair question keeps the same main identifier, such as `Q2 재시도`. Use no more than two repairs on the same link before giving the needed explanation and moving to a new probe.

Use confidence once only when it is diagnostically useful, such as a high-confidence misconception or a likely guessed answer. Do not turn the test into repeated self-rating.

Do not count five correct definitions as proof of competence. At least one question must require discrimination between confusable cases and one must require an authentic constraint or tradeoff. When the answer reveals a missing prerequisite rather than a misconception, teach that prerequisite briefly instead of escalating the trickiness of the question.

Be direct about shallow reasoning, but judge causal content rather than terminology or formatting. Stop early when the learner's stated goal is narrow and the relevant gap is already clear; do not continue all five dimensions solely for ceremony unless the learner requested a complete five-question audit.

At the end, separate current independent application from delayed retention. A five-question same-session result does not by itself prove durable memory.
